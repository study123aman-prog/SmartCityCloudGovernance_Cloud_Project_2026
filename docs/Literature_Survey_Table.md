# Literature Survey Table

The following table summarizes the 15 research papers surveyed for the Smart City Cloud Governance project, detailing the method, dataset, advantages, limitations, and identified research gap for each study.

| No. | Paper | Method | Dataset | Advantages | Limitations | Research Gap |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Digital-Twin-Based Fire Safety Management Framework for Smart Buildings | Conceptual/qualitative study exploring Digital Twin (DT) technology adoption for fire safety management (FSM); reviews DT architecture, AI engine and user-interface components for fire evacuation and FSE maintenance operations. | Practitioner survey/interviews + literature review (Survey / Not experimental) | Maps DT application areas across evacuation and maintenance; provides an industry-readiness roadmap; highlights DT dynamic-modelling and spatial-awareness potential. | Conceptual framework only, not implemented or tested on a real building. Inferred limitation: no automated policy-interpretation or compliance-decision layer; effectiveness in real emergencies not validated. | Framework stops at visualization/decision-support (Detection/Prediction/Visualization); no mechanism translates a detected fire-safety state into an enforceable governance action or a verified corrective outcome. |
| 2 | AIoT-powered building digital twin for smart firefighting and super real-time fire forecast | AIoT-integrated Digital Twin combining a wireless sensor-array network with an ADLSTM (attention-based deep LSTM) model that transforms discrete point-sensor readings into continuous spatiotemporal temperature fields and forecasts fire spread ~60 s ahead, hosted on a cloud platform. | Full-scale three-storey building fire experiments at a Hong Kong Fire Services training academy (Real-world / Experimental) | Super real-time fire-scene reconstruction; validated on full-scale live fire tests; forecasts hazardous regions to directly guide firefighter operations; strong real-time and spatial-awareness capability. | Focused solely on physical fire-spread prediction. Inferred limitation: no regulatory/compliance reasoning and no link to enforcement, accountability or audit workflows; high sensor-infrastructure dependency. | Excellent detection/forecast/visualization layer, but purely physics- and sensor-driven; it does not extend into policy interpretation, compliance-state tracking or governance decision-making. |
| 3 | Integration of Industry 4.0 Technologies in Fire and Safety Management | Review synthesizing IoT, AI, cloud computing and BIM integration in fire safety management under ISO/IEC 30141 and IEEE 802.15.4 interoperability standards; discusses predictive analytics for early detection and risk management. | Literature review / secondary sources (Not specified) | Broad coverage of Industry 4.0 enablers; highlights interoperability standards for heterogeneous fire-safety devices; identifies the cloud-based storage/prediction trend. | Review-only, no original experimental validation. Inferred limitation: no discussion of automated enforcement, escalation, or accountability mechanisms once a hazard is identified. | Surveys detection/monitoring technologies extensively but does not examine how fire-safety regulations are operationalised into enforceable, auditable governance workflows. |
| 4 | Predicting smoke hazards and burning fuel via smart building fire sensor network and dual-agent deep learning | Dual-agent deep-learning framework that uses a sparse in-building fire-sensor network to jointly predict smoke-hazard level and burning-fuel amount in complex building floorplans in real time. | Complex building floorplan fire simulations (Simulation data) | Real-time hazard quantification from sparse sensors; supports emergency-response decision-making; dual-task learning improves accuracy over single-task baselines. | Simulation-only validation; no real-building trial reported. Inferred limitation: no link between predicted hazard levels and regulatory thresholds, required corrective action, or verified compliance outcomes. | Strong hazard-quantification capability, but no mechanism connects the predicted risk level to a governance decision, a corrective-action deadline, or an audit trail. |
| 5 | Integration of Proactive Building Fire Risk Management in the Building Construction Sector: A Conceptual Framework to Understand the Existing Condition | Comprehensive literature review building a conceptual framework for Proactive Building Fire Risk Management (PBFRM) integration across the building-construction lifecycle and institutional actors. | Literature review (Not specified / secondary) | Identifies systemic and institutional research gaps in fire-risk governance; frames fire safety as a cross-lifecycle, multi-stakeholder process problem rather than a pure technology problem. | Conceptual; no empirical validation. Inferred limitation: does not propose a technical/computational mechanism for enforcement or continuous compliance tracking. | Explicitly identifies that existing fire-risk management remains reactive and fragmented across regulatory bodies, but stops short of proposing an automated, cloud-based governance mechanism to close that fragmentation. |
| 6 | An Automated Fire Code Compliance Checking Jointly Using Building Information Models and Natural Language Processing | BIM + NLP pipeline decomposing Automated Compliance Checking (ACC) into building-model parsing, NLP-based code-knowledge translation, and automated spatial/geometric compliance-result reporting. | BIM models + fire-code regulation text (Experimental — building model test cases) | Automates spatial-geometry checks that previously required manual review; decomposes ACC into reusable subtasks; improves checking consistency. | Limited to design-stage BIM compliance, not operational/post-occupancy monitoring. Inferred limitation: no continuous compliance state and no verification of corrective action after a violation is found. | Automated compliance stops at design-time rule checking; no mechanism exists for ongoing operational compliance, evidence-based verification, or escalation once the building is occupied. |
| 7 | A BIM-Based Automated Code Compliance Checking System in Malaysian Fire Safety Regulations: A User-Friendly Approach | Semantic mark-up and classification of regulatory clauses translated into a BIM-based rule-checking system (BIMSMACC) via a visual-programming language; validated through structured expert scoring. | Malaysian fire-safety regulation text + BIM proof-of-concept model; expert-validation survey (Experimental + Survey) | User-friendly semi-automated regulation-to-rule translation; high expert-validation scores (4.09-4.96/5); improves designer accessibility to code compliance. | Semi-automated — still requires regulatory-expert participation. Inferred limitation: single-jurisdiction rule set; no continuous/post-design monitoring; no enforcement-decision logic. | Demonstrates regulation-to-rule translation feasibility but confines it to design-stage review; does not extend to runtime compliance state, risk-adaptive enforcement, or escalation. |
| 8 | Comprehensive Building Fire Risk Prediction Using Machine Learning and Stacking Ensemble Methods | Stacking ensemble of 16 machine-learning algorithms trained on 34 building, land and demographic variables to classify buildings into five fire-risk grades. | Government fire-incident and building-registry records, Republic of Korea (Real-world / Government) | Empirically validated against real fire occurrences (highest-risk grade covers 22% of buildings but accounts for 54% of actual fires); supports inspection prioritisation and insurance risk pricing. | Produces a static risk-grade snapshot, not a continuously updated state. Inferred limitation: no mechanism linking the risk grade to a mandated corrective action, deadline, or verification step. | Strong risk-scoring capability, but the score is not operationalised into an enforceable, time-tracked governance decision (no deadlines, escalation path, or audit trail). |
| 9 | An ontology-based approach of automatic compliance checking for structural fire safety requirements | Domain ontology encoding structural fire-safety regulatory requirements; automated reasoning/inference engines instantiate building data against the ontology to simulate compliance checking. | Building data instances mapped into the ontology (Experimental / case-study building models) | One of the first formal machine-readable schemas specifically for structural fire safety; enables automatic inference-based checking; extensible to other regulatory domains. | Authors explicitly note that only a few key fire-safety requirements are integrated; comprehensive representation of fire safety as an integral design aspect is largely absent from prior ontologies. | Provides the ontology/rule layer for a single compliance snapshot, but stops at 'is this compliant now' — no lifecycle tracking of violations, corrective deadlines, escalation, or audit accountability. |
| 10 | A Knowledge Graph-Based Approach for Construction Safety Hazards Management and Rectification Measures Intelligent Recommendation | Four-step knowledge-graph pipeline: safety-hazard ontology construction; LLM-based (ChatGLM-6B) entity extraction from inspection text; knowledge fusion/inference; semantic retrieval and rectification-measure recommendation. | Textual and semi-structured construction-site inspection records (Real-world / Government-style inspection data) | Combines LLM extraction with knowledge-graph reasoning; recommends concrete rectification measures, not just hazard detection; improves reuse of inspection knowledge. | Focused on general construction-site hazards, not fire-specific regulation. Inferred limitation: no evidence-based verification that a recommended rectification was actually carried out; no automatic escalation logic. | Recommends corrective actions but does not close the loop with verification of whether the action was actually performed, nor does it escalate unresolved hazards automatically. |
| 11 | Leveraging large language models for BIM-based automated compliance checking | LLM-based multi-agent artifact (Design Science Research) that directly interprets natural-language regulations, extracts BIM data, executes rule checks, and auto-generates compliance reports including the LLM's step-by-step reasoning. | Norwegian building regulations (TEK17 clauses) + BIM data; development set n=87, unseen test set n=43 (Experimental) | High accuracy (97% F1 rule interpretation, 98% F1 rule execution); removes the manual rule-digitisation bottleneck; generates a human-readable reasoning trace. | Single-jurisdiction (Norwegian) regulation set tested. Inferred limitation: no continuous/operational compliance tracking, no downstream enforcement, escalation, or verified-corrective-action workflow. | State-of-the-art regulation-to-rule interpretation, but purely a one-shot design-stage checker — it does not maintain a compliance state, trigger enforcement decisions, or verify corrective evidence over time. |
| 12 | Interpretable AI for Smart City Cloud Security: A Model Context Protocol Framework for Real-Time IoT Threat Detection | Random Forest anomaly classifier combined with a Model Context Protocol (MCP) 'context-broker' layer that structures detections into JSON documents interpreted by an LLM (GPT-4) to generate role-adapted, evidence-grounded threat narratives; integrated with SIEM (Elasticsearch/ECS). | 1.9 million authentic AWS CloudTrail events including a verified 1.3-million-event real cryptomining attack campaign (Real-world) | Sub-13ms real-time detection; 96.8% detection on a verified real attack; produces a human-auditable, evidence-grounded reasoning trail directly relevant to accountability/audit governance; role-specific outputs for multi-stakeholder decision-making. | Evaluated only on cloud control-plane (AWS CloudTrail) data, not the building/fire-safety domain. Inferred limitation: the framework is security-monitoring oriented and has not been adapted to fire-safety regulatory compliance or corrective-action verification. | Demonstrates a working cloud-governance, audit-trail and interpretability architecture (IAM-adjacent, SIEM-integrated) that is architecturally transferable to fire-safety cloud governance, but has not been applied to a compliance/enforcement domain such as building fire safety. |
| 13 | Artificial intelligence assisted IoT-fog based framework for emergency fire response in smart buildings | IoT-Fog computing architecture combined with AI models for early fire detection and emergency-response decision support in smart buildings; the fog layer reduces latency relative to pure cloud processing. | Sensor/IoT data across simulated and experimental building scenarios (Experimental / Simulation) | Reduces response latency via edge/fog processing; directly references Indian urban fire-safety challenges (e.g., Delhi fires); scalable smart-building architecture. | Focused on detection/alerting latency, not regulatory compliance. Inferred limitation: no mechanism for policy interpretation, violation escalation, or cross-department (fire department / municipal corporation) coordination. | Strong latency/response-time contribution directly relevant to India's fire-safety governance context, but does not address compliance-state tracking, enforcement decisions, or accountability across multiple regulatory authorities. |
| 14 | IoT-based cloud monitoring system for building fires | Low-cost IoT sensors and real-time video surveillance feeding a cloud platform; an artificial neural network model performs fire prediction; a custom web interface supports remote monitoring and evacuation-route planning. | Laboratory and field test data (Experimental / Real-world field tests) | >95% fire-prediction accuracy; low-cost, low-computation design suitable for wide deployment; cloud-based remote monitoring supports evacuation planning. | Cloud usage limited to data storage/relay, not governance. Inferred limitation: no IAM/access-control, audit-trail, or policy-enforcement functionality despite being described as 'cloud-based'. | Illustrates that most 'cloud' fire-safety systems use the cloud purely as a data pipe, not as a governance substrate — reinforcing the gap around IAM, audit trails and accountable cloud governance for fire safety. |
| 15 | Integrating IoT Technology for Fire Risk Monitoring and Assessment in Residential Building Design | BIM-IoT framework combining PyroSim-FDS fire/evacuation simulation with an Improved Risk Index (IRI) that synthesises real-time sensor data (temperature, smoke, CO) and architectural configuration; adaptive evacuation routing. | Real-world empirical deployment on a 12-storey residential building in Wuhan, China (Real-world / Experimental) | 30% reduction in fire-detection interval, 25% reduction in evacuation time, 95.2% detection accuracy, 40% fewer manual inspections — strong real-world validated performance. | Focused on detection/evacuation, not regulatory compliance tracking. Inferred limitation: the Improved Risk Index is not tied to any legal compliance threshold, corrective-action deadline, or enforcement workflow. | Excellent real-time risk-index and evacuation optimisation, but the index operates purely as an operational safety metric — it is not connected to a compliance/enforcement decision layer or audit trail. |
