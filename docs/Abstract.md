# Abstract

Fire-safety management systems increasingly use artificial intelligence, Internet of Things sensing, digital twins, BIM-based compliance checking and cloud platforms to detect hazards, predict risk and monitor building conditions. However, an important operational failure can occur after a violation has been detected: a building owner or operator may report that the violation has been corrected and submit evidence, yet the available evidence may be incomplete, stale, contradictory, associated with the wrong physical asset, or incapable of proving that the required physical condition was actually restored. If such evidence is accepted, the digital compliance record can incorrectly transition to a resolved state while the physical safety problem remains.

This project proposes an **Intelligent Cloud-Based Fire-Safety Compliance Governance System with Remediation-Attributed Evidence Verification**. The proposed research introduces a regulation-conditioned proof mechanism in which selected fire-safety requirements are represented as machine-checkable proof conditions associated with a specific physical safety asset. When a violation is identified and remediation is reported, the system evaluates subsequent evidence using asset identity, temporal validity, physical-state requirements, evidence sufficiency and consistency checks. Instead of treating evidence submission as equivalent to successful remediation, the system evaluates whether the evidence supports attribution of the observed compliant condition to the recorded remediation of the correct asset.

When the required remediation proof cannot yet be established, the system maintains the case in an unresolved proof state and can initiate targeted verification, such as requesting a new image, querying a simulated sensor, executing a functional test or requesting human inspection. New evidence updates the proof state. The violation is allowed to transition to `VERIFIED_RESOLVED` only when the required remediation-attribution conditions are satisfied; otherwise the system maintains the unresolved/failed state and can initiate additional verification, corrective action or escalation.

The research contribution is centered on **remediation attribution**, rather than on generic fire detection, AI, cloud computing, evidence collection or audit logging. The main hypothesis is that conditioning automated compliance closure on remediation-attributed physical evidence can reduce false compliance resolution compared with simpler evidence-acceptance and confidence-threshold approaches. The proposed evaluation will compare baseline and proposed workflows using metrics including false-resolution rate, remediation-attribution accuracy, verification effort, time to verified closure, human intervention rate, evidence redundancy and contradiction detection.

A student-scale prototype can be implemented using a limited set of fire-safety violation classes—such as blocked emergency exits, fire-door functional failures and fire-extinguisher certification issues—together with simulated sensor data and controlled evidence scenarios. AWS can provide the cloud infrastructure for data ingestion, computation, evidence storage, compliance-state management, workflow orchestration, security and monitoring. AWS is treated as an implementation platform rather than the source of invention novelty.

The invention is a **candidate research hypothesis and not a confirmed patent claim**. Existing literature and patents already disclose substantial portions of operational fire-safety compliance, evidence verification, post-remediation verification, compliance-state tracking and adaptive testing. The project therefore focuses on determining whether the specific coupling of a recorded violation, physical asset identity, remediation event, expected physical effect, time/evidence linkage and remediation-attribution decision provides a sufficiently differentiated technical mechanism for research and possible future patent examination.
