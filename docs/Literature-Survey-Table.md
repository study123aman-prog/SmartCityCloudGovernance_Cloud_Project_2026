# Literature Survey & Comparative Analysis Table

## Project Title
**IgnisCore — Intelligent Fire Hazard Monitoring System: Cloud-Native Multi-Zone Fire Hazard Intelligence, Graph-Based Risk Propagation & Dynamic Evacuation Decision Support System**

---

### Overview

The following comparative matrix analyzes 15 high-impact peer-reviewed research papers published between 2023 and 2025 across leading academic publication venues: **IEEE, Springer, Elsevier, ACM, Wiley, MDPI, and Nature (Scopus Indexed)**.

The surveyed literature spans five essential technical pillars of modern fire safety and emergency response:
1. **IoT & Edge-Assisted Telemetry Infrastructure** (Microcontroller sensing, virtual telemetry, and cloud ingestion)
2. **Machine Learning & Pre-Ignition Hazard Prediction** (Supervised classifiers, electrical overload detection, and explainable safety rule baselines)
3. **Graph-Theoretic Hazard Diffusion & Digital Twin Building Modeling** (Topological networks, spatiotemporal diffusion, and discrete-time cascades)
4. **Dynamic Hazard-Adaptive Evacuation Egress Routing** (Dynamic Dijkstra, A*, crowd simulation, and continuous hazard-cost penalization)
5. **Multi-Source Risk Fusion & Resilient Cloud Architecture** (Wildland-Urban Interface meteorology, Canadian FWI, serverless event pipelines, and local-first operational autonomy)

Each entry details the paper citation, core methodology, dataset/platform used, key advantages, inherent limitations, and the specific research gap addressed by the **IgnisCore** platform.

### Synthesis of Research Trends & IgnisCore Positioning

Across the 15 surveyed works published between 2023 and 2025 in IEEE, Springer, Elsevier, ACM, Wiley, MDPI, and Nature:

1. **Hardware Limitations vs. Virtual Simulation**: Conventional IoT works (Chen et al., Al-Khafaji et al.) remain tethered to physical sensor microcontrollers that fail under heat, demonstrating the necessity of IgnisCore's 100% software-only virtual telemetry engine.
2. **Predictive Capability vs. Reactive Alarms**: Prior machine learning works (Al-Smadi et al., Sharma et al.) confirm that evaluating pre-ignition thermal and electrical precursors provides critical early warning minutes before combustion occurs.
3. **Graph Topology vs. Isolated Point Sensing**: Advanced modeling works (Zhou et al., Zhang et al., Song et al.) prove that topological graph networks are vastly superior to disconnected sensor charts for modeling multi-hop hazard diffusion.
4. **Dynamic Egress vs. Static Signage**: Dynamic routing research (Wang et al., Ray et al., Li et al.) demonstrates that dynamically weighting evacuation graph edges based on real-time hazard severity prevents directing occupants into lethal corridors.
5. **Holistic Risk Fusion vs. Siloed Domains**: Recent risk assessment studies (Meng et al., Park et al.) emphasize the urgent need to bridge wildland meteorology (Canadian FWI) and indoor building parameters into unified multi-source hazard indices.
6. **Cloud-Native Scalability & Local Resiliency**: Cloud architectures (Sunkpho et al., Kumar et al.) validate that combining local-first operational autonomy with serverless cloud-native pipelines (AWS IoT Core, Lambda, DynamoDB, SNS) provides optimal reliability for life-safety systems.
